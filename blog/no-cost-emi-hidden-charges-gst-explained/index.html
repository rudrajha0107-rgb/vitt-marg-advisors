No-cost EMI is not free: the GST, fee and lost discount explained with a Rs 60,000 phone

Personal FinanceNovember 2026By CMA Sudarshan Rudra Jha

"No-cost EMI" sounds like the bank is lending you money for free. It is not. The interest still exists. It is simply hidden inside a discount that the seller gives, and you pay other charges on top of it that the word "no-cost" does not mention.

The short version. On a Rs 60,000 phone bought on a 6-month no-cost EMI, a buyer in our example pays about Rs 692 more than the sticker price through GST on interest and a processing fee. If the same card also offered a Rs 3,000 instant discount for full payment, the total cost of choosing EMI rises to Rs 3,692.

How a no-cost EMI actually works

Banks do not lend at zero interest. In a no-cost EMI, the seller or brand gives you an upfront discount equal to the interest the bank will charge. The bank then adds that interest back through your EMIs. The two cancel out, so the EMIs add up to the sticker price, and it looks free.

A worked example on a Rs 60,000 phone

This example assumes a 6-month EMI, 15% annual interest and a Rs 199 processing fee. Your bank and offer will have different figures.

The three costs that "no-cost" hides

1. GST on the hidden interest

Interest on a credit card EMI is a financial service, and the bank charges 18% GST on it. The seller's discount covers the interest, but not the GST on that interest. That GST shows up in your card statement, usually in the first or second month.

2. The processing fee

Most banks charge a one-time processing fee for converting a purchase into EMI, often between Rs 99 and Rs 299. GST at 18% is added on this fee too.

3. The discount you give up

This is usually the biggest cost. Many sales offer an instant discount for full payment on a particular card, and that discount is often not available with no-cost EMI. If you choose EMI, you lose it.

Five checks before you press "No-cost EMI"

  1. Ask for the processing fee in rupees, and add 18% GST to it.
  2. Work out the interest: the difference between the sticker price and the discounted price is roughly the interest. Add 18% GST to that.
  3. Compare with the full-payment price on the same card, including any instant discount or cashback.
  4. Check whether pre-closing the EMI attracts a foreclosure fee.
  5. Make sure the EMI amount fits your monthly budget. A missed EMI attracts late fees and interest at the card's normal rate, which is far higher.

When no-cost EMI does make sense

It can still be the better option if there is no full-payment discount on offer, the processing fee is zero, and you need to keep cash in hand for something more important. In that case the only extra cost is the GST on interest, which is small.

Frequently asked questions

Is no-cost EMI really free?

No. The bank still charges interest. The seller gives you an equal discount, so the EMIs add up to the sticker price, but you still pay 18% GST on that interest and usually a processing fee with GST.

Why is GST charged on a no-cost EMI?

GST at 18% applies to the interest and the processing fee because they are financial services provided by the bank. The seller's discount covers the interest itself but not the GST on it.

How much extra does a no-cost EMI cost?

It depends on the price, tenure, interest rate and fee. In our example of a Rs 60,000 phone over 6 months at 15% interest with a Rs 199 fee, the extra is about Rs 692, or Rs 3,692 if a Rs 3,000 full-payment discount is also given up.

Is no-cost EMI better than paying in full?

Only when there is no full-payment discount, the processing fee is low or zero, and you need to keep cash in hand. Otherwise, paying in full on a card with an instant discount is usually cheaper.
