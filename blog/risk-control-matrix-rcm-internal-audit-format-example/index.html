Risk Control Matrix (RCM): what it is, a simple format and a worked example

Internal AuditNovember 2026By CMA Sudarshan Rudra Jha

Ask any internal auditor what they look at first, and most will say the Risk Control Matrix, or RCM. It is a simple table that lists, for each business process, what can go wrong and what the company does to stop it.

Companies need an RCM because the directors are responsible for internal financial controls (IFC), and for larger companies the statutory auditor also has to report on whether those controls work. Without an RCM, there is nothing written down to test.

What goes into an RCM

ColumnWhat to write
Process and sub-processFor example, purchase-to-pay, vendor creation
RiskWhat can go wrong, in one line
ControlWhat the company does to stop it
Control ownerThe person responsible, by designation
FrequencyEach transaction, daily, monthly, yearly
TypePreventive or detective; manual or automated
Key control?Yes if failure could cause a material error
EvidenceThe document that proves the control happened

Do the same for the other main processes: order-to-cash, inventory, payroll, fixed assets, treasury and financial closing.

How an internal auditor tests the RCM

  1. Walkthrough: follow one real transaction from start to end to confirm the control exists as written.
  2. Test of design: check that the control, if it works, would actually stop the risk.
  3. Test of operating effectiveness: pick a sample (for example 25 invoices) and check the evidence for each one.
  4. Report: list the gaps, rate them high, medium or low, and agree on a fix with a date and an owner.

Common mistakes

  • Writing the policy instead of the control. "Payments are approved" is not enough. Who approves, in which system, and above what amount?
  • Too many controls marked as key. Keep the key ones to what really matters.
  • No evidence column. If nobody can show proof, the control will fail the test.
  • Not updating the RCM after an ERP change or a new process.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Risk Control Matrix (RCM)?

It is a table that lists, for each business process, the risks of what can go wrong and the controls the company uses to stop them, with the owner, frequency, type and evidence of each control.

Why does a company need an RCM?

Directors are responsible for internal financial controls, and for larger companies the statutory auditor also reports on them. The RCM is the written record that both use to test those controls.

What is the difference between preventive and detective controls?

A preventive control stops an error before it happens, like a system blocking a duplicate invoice. A detective control finds it afterwards, like a monthly reconciliation.

How is an RCM tested in internal audit?

By a walkthrough of one transaction, a test of design, and a test of operating effectiveness on a sample, followed by a report of the gaps.
