ITR-U (updated return): last date, extra tax and who can file in 2026

Income TaxNovember 2026By CMA Sudarshan Rudra Jha

Say you filed your income tax return two years ago and later realised you forgot to show interest from an FD, a freelance payment or a property sale. Or you never filed a return for that year. The normal deadlines for a belated or revised return are long gone. What now?

This is exactly what ITR-U, the updated return, is for. It lets you correct an old return, or file one you missed, up to 4 years (48 months) after the end of that year. You pay the tax you missed, the interest on it, and an extra amount as a fine for being late.

From 1 April 2026, the new Income Tax Act, 2025 applies. The updated return rule is now in section 263 of the new Act. Under the old Act it was section 139(8A). The idea is the same.

ITR-U last date and extra tax

The extra tax depends on how late you file. It is worked out on the tax you missed plus the interest on it.

When you file ITR-UExtra tax you pay
Within 1 year from the end of the tax year25%
Within 2 years50%
Within 3 years60%
Within 4 years (last chance)70%

Example: you missed income on which the tax and interest come to ₹40,000. If you file ITR-U within the first year, you pay ₹40,000 plus 25%, which is ₹10,000, so ₹50,000 in total. If you wait till the fourth year, the extra becomes ₹28,000. Filing early saves real money.

Who can file ITR-U

  • You filed a return but left out some income.
  • You did not file a return at all for that year, and you now owe tax.
  • You claimed a loss earlier and want to reduce it. This is allowed from 2026.

Who cannot file ITR-U

  • You want a refund, or a bigger refund. ITR-U is only for paying more tax, not for getting money back.
  • You want to show less income or less tax than your earlier return.
  • The tax department has already started a search or survey for that year, or started prosecution.
  • The assessment or reassessment for that year is already complete.

Got a reassessment notice? You can still file ITR-U

Earlier, once a reassessment notice came, ITR-U was no longer allowed. From 2026 this has changed. If you get a reassessment notice, you can still file an updated return for that year within the time given in the notice. You pay 10% more as extra tax in this case, so the rates become 35%, 60%, 70% and 80%. In many cases this still works out cheaper and quicker than fighting the reassessment and paying a penalty.

How to file ITR-U: 4 steps

  1. Work out the income you missed, the tax on it and the interest.
  2. Add the extra tax (25% to 70%) and pay the full amount through a tax challan first.
  3. Log in to the income tax e-filing portal, choose "Updated Return", pick the year and the correct ITR form.
  4. Enter the reason and the challan details, submit, and e-verify the return.

Keep the challan and the working safe. If the department asks later, these show that you corrected the mistake yourself.

Frequently asked questions

What is the last date to file ITR-U?

You can file ITR-U up to 48 months, that is 4 years, from the end of the tax year you are correcting.

What is the penalty for filing ITR-U?

There is no separate penalty. You pay extra tax of 25%, 50%, 60% or 70% of the tax and interest, depending on whether you file within 1, 2, 3 or 4 years.

Can I get a refund through ITR-U?

No. ITR-U can only be used to pay more tax. It cannot create a refund, increase a refund, or reduce the tax you declared earlier.

Can I file ITR-U if I never filed a return for that year?

Yes, as long as you have tax to pay for that year and none of the restrictions apply.

Can I file ITR-U after getting an income tax notice?

From 2026, yes, after a reassessment notice, within the time given in the notice, with 10% extra on top of the normal rates. Not if a search, survey or prosecution has started.
