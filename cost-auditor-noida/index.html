Do you need cost records or a cost audit?

Section 148 of the Companies Act, 2013 and the Companies (Cost Records and Audit) Rules, 2014 set two separate tests. Both use turnover of the preceding financial year.

RequirementApplies when
Maintain cost records (Form CRA-1)Your product or service is listed in the Rules and overall turnover is ₹35 crore or more
Cost audit: regulated sectors (Table A)
telecom, electricity, petroleum products, drugs and pharma, fertilisers, sugar and industrial alcohol		Overall turnover ₹50 crore or more, and turnover of the covered product or service ₹25 crore or more
Cost audit: non-regulated sectors (Table B)
steel, automobiles, chemicals, machinery, electrical and electronic equipment, and others		Overall turnover ₹100 crore or more, and turnover of the covered product or service ₹35 crore or more

Outside the audit: micro and small enterprises under the MSMED Act, companies whose export earnings in foreign exchange are more than 75% of total revenue, units in a Special Economic Zone, and captive power generation. Some of these still have to keep cost records.

Many manufacturers in Noida's industrial sectors, between ₹35 crore and ₹100 crore turnover, need cost records but have never kept them. That is the most common gap we find.

Who can be your cost auditor

  • Only a Cost Accountant in practice, or a firm of Cost Accountants, can conduct a cost audit.
  • Your statutory auditor cannot also be your cost auditor. The two audits are kept separate by law.
  • The same cost auditor can be re-appointed, but CRA-2 has to be filed afresh every year.

Frequently asked questions

Our turnover is ₹60 crore and we make steel products. Do we need a cost audit?

Most likely you need to keep cost records, because turnover is above ₹35 crore. A cost audit for a non-regulated product like steel starts only at ₹100 crore overall turnover and ₹35 crore for the product. We check the exact product headings before confirming.

We are an MSME. Does cost audit apply to us?

Micro and small enterprises under the MSMED Act are outside the Rules. Since April 2025, a small enterprise can have turnover up to ₹100 crore, so check your current Udyam classification. A medium enterprise is not exempt.

We missed CRA-2 or CRA-4. What now?

File it as soon as possible with the additional fee. The fee rises the longer it waits, and the company and its officers can face penalties under the Act. We prepare the pending resolutions and forms and file them together.

Can our statutory auditor do the cost audit as well?

No. The law does not allow the company's statutory auditor to be its cost auditor. You need a separate Cost Accountant in practice.
