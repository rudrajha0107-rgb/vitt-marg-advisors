UPI charges for shopkeepers: 0.4% above ₹2,000, now delayed. What is proposed and how to prepare

BusinessNovember 2026Vitt-Marg Advisors

UPI has been free for shops since it began. In September 2026, NPCI announced a merchant discount rate (MDR) on larger UPI payments to merchants, due from 15 October 2026. Before that date it was postponed to after Diwali, and as of late November 2026 no new start date has been notified. Reports from 19 November suggest it could be pushed further. Here is what was announced, so that you are not caught off guard when it does start.

In one line: customers will not pay anything. The shop or business receiving the money would pay 0.4% on UPI payments above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300 a payment. Payments up to ₹2,000, and transfers between people, stay free. Small shops with UPI QR receipts up to ₹1 lakh a month are exempt.

What was announced

Type of paymentProposed charge to the merchant
Person to person, any amountFree
Payment to a merchant, up to ₹2,000Free
Payment to a merchant, above ₹2,0000.4%, capped at ₹300 a payment
Railways, telecom, insurance and fuel, above ₹2,000Flat ₹5 a payment
Mutual funds, brokers and other capital-market payments0.02%, capped at ₹300
Small merchants with UPI QR receipts up to ₹1 lakh a monthExempt

NPCI has said that payments up to ₹2,000 make up more than 95% of merchant UPI payments, so most small-ticket buying will not be touched.

For a showroom or distributor collecting ₹20 lakh a month in UPI payments of ₹10,000 each, the cost would be about ₹8,000 a month. That is still far below the 1.5% to 2% charged on most cards.

Can the shop pass the charge to the customer?

Banks and NPCI have said the merchant must bear it and should not add a surcharge for paying by UPI. Payment apps are also not allowed to charge users a platform fee for this.

The tax and accounting side

  • The MDR will be deducted by your bank or payment aggregator from the settlement. Book the full sale value as turnover and the MDR as a bank charge. Do not show the net amount as the sale.
  • Bank charges such as MDR generally carry 18% GST. If you are registered, the GST on the charge is available as input tax credit, so download the monthly invoice from your bank or aggregator.
  • The MDR is a business expense and is deductible for income tax.

How to prepare

  • Ask your bank whether your QR qualifies for the small-merchant exemption.
  • Look at how much of your UPI money comes in payments above ₹2,000. That is the part that will cost you.
  • For large B2B receipts, NEFT and RTGS remain free at most banks.
  • Watch for the final notification; the rates above may change before the rollout.

Frequently asked questions

Will customers pay a charge on UPI payments?

No. Under the announced framework, the charge falls only on the merchant receiving the payment. Person-to-person transfers stay free at any amount.

From when will UPI MDR start?

It was announced for 15 October 2026 and then deferred to after Diwali. As of late November 2026, no new start date has been notified.

How much is the UPI MDR for payments above ₹2,000?

0.4% of the payment, capped at ₹300 a payment. Railways, telecom, insurance and fuel pay a flat ₹5, and capital-market payments pay 0.02%.

Are small shopkeepers exempt from UPI charges?

Yes. Merchants receiving up to ₹1 lakh a month through UPI QR codes are exempt under the announced framework. Confirm eligibility with your bank.

Is GST charged on UPI MDR?

Bank charges such as MDR generally attract 18% GST. A GST-registered business can claim it as input tax credit using the invoice from its bank or payment aggregator.
