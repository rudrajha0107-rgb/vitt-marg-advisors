Loan against mutual funds: how to get cash without selling your investments

Personal FinanceNovember 202670-second videoVitt-Marg Advisors

Suppose you have ₹5 lakh in mutual funds and suddenly need ₹2 lakh. The first thought for most people is to sell the funds. There is another way: you can pledge the funds and take a loan against them, so you get the money and your investment keeps running.

The short version. In a loan against mutual funds, your units stay in your name and the lender only locks them. Against equity funds you usually get about half of the value. Pledging is not a sale, so no capital gains tax arises now, but you do pay interest and EMI on the loan.

How a loan against mutual funds works

  1. You pledge your units with a bank or lender, in the same way that gold is pledged in a gold loan. This is usually done online through the registrar (CAMS or KFintech) with an OTP.
  2. The lender marks a lien on the units. They remain in your name and keep earning returns, but you cannot sell them until the loan is closed.
  3. The money is credited to your bank account, either as a loan or as an overdraft limit that you draw as needed.
  4. You repay with interest, and once the loan is closed the lien is removed and the units are free again.

How much money can you get?

For equity funds, lenders usually lend about half of the current value. So ₹5 lakh of equity funds can support a limit of roughly ₹2.5 lakh, and the ₹2 lakh you need can come straight into your bank account. Debt funds usually get a higher share. The exact limit depends on the lender and on which funds you pledge.

Five benefits of pledging instead of selling

  1. You do not have to sell your funds. The units remain yours.
  2. Your money stays invested in the market, so the growth continues.
  3. No capital gains tax now, because pledging is not a sale.
  4. Interest is often lower than on a personal loan, although it depends on the lender and your terms.
  5. Money comes quickly in an emergency, often within a day or two once the pledge is done.

What to keep in mind before you pledge

  • If the market falls, the value of your pledged units falls too. The lender can then ask you to add more units or repay part of the loan, and if you do not, it can sell some units, which may also create a tax liability.
  • ELSS units cannot be pledged during their three-year lock-in.
  • Compare the full cost, including the interest rate, processing fee and any prepayment charges, with the tax you would pay if you sold instead.

An investment is built for emergencies. The real question is whether to break it or to use it, so understand both options and then decide.

Frequently asked questions

What is a loan against mutual funds?

You pledge your mutual fund units with a bank or lender, much like gold is pledged in a gold loan. The units stay in your name, and the lender only marks a lien (a lock) on them until the loan is repaid.

How much loan can I get against mutual funds?

For equity funds, lenders usually give about half of the current value, so ₹5 lakh of equity funds can support a limit of roughly ₹2.5 lakh. Debt funds usually get a higher share. The exact limit depends on the lender and the fund.

Do I pay capital gains tax when I pledge mutual funds?

No. Pledging is not a sale, so no capital gains tax arises when you pledge units. Tax arises only when units are sold or redeemed, including if the lender sells them because the loan is not serviced.

Is it better to sell mutual funds or take a loan against them?

Selling ends the investment and may trigger capital gains tax, but there is no loan to repay. Pledging keeps the investment running, but you pay interest and EMI. Compare the loan cost with the expected return and the tax you would pay on selling before you decide.

Can ELSS funds be pledged for a loan?

ELSS units cannot be pledged while they are in their three-year lock-in. Once the lock-in ends, they can usually be pledged like other equity funds.
