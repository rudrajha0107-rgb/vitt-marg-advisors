Income tax for freelancers and YouTubers in India: a complete guide for 2026

Income TaxNovember 2026By CMA Sudarshan Rudra Jha

Freelancing and content creation are now full-time careers for lakhs of people in India. The income is taxable like any other business or professional income, and the rules are friendlier than many expect. Most freelancers and creators can use a presumptive scheme, keep minimal books and pay tax on only part of their receipts. This guide explains the choices.

The short version. Freelance and creator income is business or professional income, not salary. A freelancer in a listed profession can declare 50% of receipts as income under the presumptive scheme. Creators usually fall under the business scheme at 6% or 8% of receipts. GST registration is needed above ₹20 lakh of turnover, and export income still counts towards that limit.

From tax year 2026-27, both schemes sit in Section 58 of the Income-tax Act, 2025. The rates and limits remain the same. You can always declare a higher income than the presumptive figure. If you want to declare less, you need to keep full books and may need an audit.

Whether a YouTuber or influencer is a "film artist" or a business depends on the facts. Most creators file under the business scheme, which usually gives a lower taxable figure anyway.

Expenses: presumptive or actual?

Under a presumptive scheme you cannot claim expenses separately, because the 50%, 8% or 6% figure is treated as your profit after all expenses. If your real costs are higher, for example a creator who spends a lot on equipment, editors and travel, keeping books and claiming actual expenses may give a lower tax. Compare both before you file.

GST on freelance and YouTube income

  • Registration is needed when your aggregate turnover of services crosses ₹20 lakh in a financial year (₹10 lakh in some special category states).
  • Foreign clients and YouTube or AdSense income paid from abroad are usually treated as export of services. Export is zero-rated, so no GST is charged on it if you file a Letter of Undertaking (LUT), but it still counts towards the ₹20 lakh limit.
  • Indian brand deals attract 18% GST once you are registered.

TDS, brand gifts and foreign tax

  • Indian clients deduct TDS on professional fees, usually 10%, or 2% for technical services. It shows in your Form 26AS and is adjusted against your final tax.
  • Free products from brands are taxable. If a brand gives you products or benefits worth more than ₹20,000 in a year, it must deduct 10% TDS on the value, and the value is your income.
  • US tax on YouTube earnings. Google withholds US tax on earnings from US viewers. You can claim credit for it in India by filing Form 67 before your return.

Advance tax

If your tax for the year after TDS is ₹10,000 or more, you must pay advance tax. Those using a presumptive scheme can pay the full amount in one instalment by 15 March. Others pay in four instalments; see our advance tax guide. Missing it leads to interest under Sections 234B and 234C.

Example

A freelance web developer receives ₹18 lakh in the year, all through bank transfers. Under the professional scheme, 50% or ₹9 lakh is taxable income. In the new regime that is below ₹12 lakh, so the tax is nil after the rebate, and any TDS deducted by clients comes back as a refund when the return is filed.

Frequently asked questions

How is freelance income taxed in India?

Freelance income is taxed as business or professional income. A freelancer in a listed profession, such as IT, engineering or consultancy, can declare 50% of gross receipts as income under the presumptive scheme if receipts are up to ₹50 lakh, or ₹75 lakh if cash receipts are 5% or less.

Which ITR should a YouTuber file?

A YouTuber using the presumptive scheme can file ITR-4 if total income is up to ₹50 lakh. If they keep full books, claim actual expenses or have income above ₹50 lakh, they file ITR-3.

Is GST applicable on YouTube income?

YouTube and AdSense income paid from abroad is usually export of services and zero-rated, so no GST is charged on it if an LUT is filed. It still counts towards the ₹20 lakh registration limit. Indian brand deals attract 18% GST once you are registered.

Are free products from brands taxable for influencers?

Yes. Products or benefits received for business are taxable income. If their value is more than ₹20,000 in a year, the brand must deduct 10% TDS on the value.
