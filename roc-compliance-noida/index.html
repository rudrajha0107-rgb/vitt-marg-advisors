Annual compliance for a private limited company

For a company with a 31 March year-end, the main annual obligations are:

Filing or eventWhen
Board meetingsAt least four a year, no more than 120 days apart (small companies and OPCs: one in each half of the calendar year, at least 90 days apart)
Annual general meeting (AGM)By 30 September (first AGM within nine months of the first year-end)
AOC-4 — financial statementsWithin 30 days of the AGM
MGT-7 / MGT-7A — annual returnWithin 60 days of the AGM
ADT-1 — auditor appointmentWithin 15 days of the AGM at which the auditor is appointed
DPT-3 — return of deposits and outstanding loansBy 30 June
MSME-1 — dues to micro and small suppliersHalf-yearly, where dues are outstanding beyond 45 days
Director disclosures (DIR-8, MBP-1)At the first board meeting of the year

If the AGM is held earlier than 30 September, the AOC-4 and MGT-7 due dates move earlier too, because both run from the AGM date.

One person companies don't hold an AGM. An OPC files AOC-4 within 180 days of the year-end and the simplified annual return (MGT-7A) afterwards.

DIR-3 KYC is now every three years

Director KYC used to be an annual filing. Since 31 March 2026, every DIN holder files DIR-3 KYC once every three financial years, through the DIR-3 KYC Web form. Changes to a director's mobile number, email or address must still be updated within 30 days.

If the KYC isn't filed when it falls due, the DIN is deactivated and a ₹5,000 fee applies to reactivate it. A deactivated DIN also blocks the director from signing the company's other filings.

Event-based filings

Beyond the annual cycle, certain events have their own forms, most due within 30 days:

  • Appointment or resignation of a director (DIR-12)
  • Allotment of new shares (PAS-3)
  • Increase in authorised capital (SH-7)
  • Change of registered office (INC-22)
  • Creation or modification of a charge for a loan (CHG-1)
  • Changes to the memorandum or articles, and certain board or shareholder resolutions (MGT-14)

What delay costs

  • ₹100 per day additional fee on late AOC-4 and MGT-7 — with no upper limit, per form.
  • Directors of a company that doesn't file financial statements or annual returns for three consecutive years are disqualified for five years — from every company where they're a director.
  • The Registrar can strike off companies that fail to file.

A company with no activity still has to file. Dormant companies are often the ones that end up with the largest late fees.

LLP compliance

LLPs file two annual forms: the annual return in Form 11 by 30 May, and the statement of accounts and solvency in Form 8 by 30 October. Late filing attracts a daily additional fee here too.

Frequently asked questions

Our company had no business this year. Do we still need to file?

Yes. AOC-4 and MGT-7 are due every year whether or not the company traded. Missing them builds up late fees of ₹100 per day per form and can lead to director disqualification.

We missed last year's filings. What can we do?

File the pending forms with the additional fees as soon as possible — the fee keeps growing daily. We'll list everything that's pending, calculate the exposure and file in the right order.

Is DIR-3 KYC still due every year?

No. Since 31 March 2026, it's filed once every three financial years. Any change in a director's contact details must still be updated within 30 days.

Can you handle ROC compliance for an LLP?

Yes — Form 11 (annual return) by 30 May, Form 8 (accounts and solvency) by 30 October, and event-based filings such as partner changes.

Do you also carry out the statutory audit?

No. A company's statutory audit must be done by an independent practising Chartered Accountant. We prepare the books and coordinate with your auditor, and handle the ROC filings once the audit is complete.
