GST on scrap from 1 November 2026: reverse charge, compulsory registration and 2% TDS

GSTNovember 2026Vitt-Marg Advisors

From 1 November 2026, the GST rules for buying and selling scrap changed in three ways at once. The changes were notified by CBIC on 27 October 2026 and hit almost every factory in Noida and Delhi NCR, because nearly every factory sells scrap and many buy it.

In one line: if you buy plastic scrap, tyre scrap, e-waste or used cooking oil from an unregistered seller, you now pay the GST under reverse charge. Every seller of these goods, and of metal scrap, must register. And when one registered business buys these goods from another, the buyer deducts 2% GST TDS.

The notifications are No. 04/2026-Central Tax (Rate) for reverse charge, No. 06/2026-Central Tax for registration, and No. 07/2026-Central Tax for TDS. The GST rate on the goods themselves has not changed. What has changed is who pays the tax, who must register, and how the payment is made.

Change 1: reverse charge when you buy from an unregistered seller

When a registered business buys any of the four new items from an unregistered person, such as a local kabadiwala or a small collector, the buyer pays the GST directly to the government. The seller does not charge GST at all.

  • The buyer issues a self-invoice within 30 days of receiving the goods.
  • The buyer pays the GST in cash through GSTR-3B. Reverse charge tax cannot be paid from input tax credit.
  • The buyer can then claim the same amount back as input tax credit, if the scrap is used for business.

Change 2: every scrap seller must now register

Earlier, a person who sold only goods taxed under reverse charge did not need GST registration. That exemption has been withdrawn for all five categories, including metal scrap. A small scrap dealer whose whole business is buying from households and selling to recyclers now needs a GST number, whatever the turnover.

For factories this matters on the selling side too. Most factories are already registered, but group companies, small job-work units and unregistered sister concerns that sell scrap should check their position now.

Change 3: 2% TDS when both sides are registered

When a registered buyer purchases any of the five categories from a registered seller, the buyer must deduct 2% GST TDS: 1% CGST and 1% SGST for a sale within the state, or 2% IGST for an inter-state sale. As with all GST TDS, it applies where the value of supply under a contract is more than ₹2.5 lakh, and it is worked out on the value without GST.

  • The buyer registers as a GST TDS deductor if not already registered, in form GST REG-07.
  • The buyer files GSTR-7 and deposits the TDS by the 10th of the next month.
  • The seller accepts the TDS on the portal, and the amount is credited to the seller's electronic cash ledger.

A worked example

A Noida manufacturer buys metal scrap worth ₹3,00,000 from a registered dealer in Ghaziabad, with GST at 18%.

ParticularsAmount (₹)
Value of scrap (contract above ₹2.5 lakh)3,00,000
GST at 18% charged by the dealer54,000
Invoice total3,54,000
TDS deducted by the buyer: 1% CGST + 1% SGST on ₹3,00,0006,000
Paid to the dealer3,48,000

The buyer deposits ₹6,000 with GSTR-7 by the 10th of the next month. The dealer accepts it and uses the ₹6,000 in the cash ledger to pay its own GST. The buyer still claims the full ₹54,000 as input tax credit, subject to the usual conditions.

If the same manufacturer had bought ₹3,00,000 of plastic scrap from an unregistered collector, the collector would charge no GST. The manufacturer would issue a self-invoice, pay the GST itself under reverse charge, and claim it back as credit.

What to do this month

  1. List every scrap you buy and sell by HSN code: 1518, 3915, 4004, Chapters 72 to 81, and 8549. The rules apply by category, so "scrap" in general is not enough.
  2. Check each supplier's GST status before paying. Registered means TDS. Unregistered means reverse charge, except for metal scrap, which was already under reverse charge.
  3. Register as a TDS deductor if you buy above ₹2.5 lakh under any contract from registered sellers.
  4. Update your accounting software for the new reverse charge entries and the TDS deduction on payment.
  5. On the selling side, check that buyers' TDS shows up in your cash ledger every month, and follow up if it does not.
  6. Plan for e-invoicing. From 1 January 2027, businesses with turnover above ₹5 crore must generate an e-invoice for reverse charge self-invoices too. See our note on e-invoicing for RCM self-invoices.

Frequently asked questions

From when do the new GST rules on scrap apply?

From 1 November 2026, under Notifications 04/2026-Central Tax (Rate), 06/2026-Central Tax and 07/2026-Central Tax, all dated 27 October 2026.

Which scrap is now under reverse charge?

Used cooking oil (1518), plastic waste and scrap (3915), tyre waste and scrap (4004) and electrical and electronic waste (8549), when a registered person buys them from an unregistered person. Metal scrap was already under reverse charge from October 2024.

Does a small scrap dealer need GST registration now?

Yes. The exemption for persons supplying only reverse charge goods no longer applies to sellers of these five categories, including metal scrap, so they must register regardless of turnover.

What is the GST TDS rate on scrap purchases?

Two percent: 1% CGST and 1% SGST, or 2% IGST, deducted by a registered buyer from a registered seller where the contract value without GST is more than ₹2.5 lakh. It is deposited with GSTR-7 by the 10th of the next month.

Does the GST rate on scrap change?

No. These notifications change who pays the tax, who must register and how TDS works. The rate on the goods stays the same.
