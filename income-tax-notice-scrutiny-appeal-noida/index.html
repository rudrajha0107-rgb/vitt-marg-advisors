The notices we handle most

NoticeWhat it means
Intimation after processing (section 143(1))The return was processed with an adjustment or a demand. Often a mismatch with AIS or Form 26AS that can be corrected.
Defective return (section 139(9))Something in the return is incomplete or inconsistent and must be fixed within the time given.
Scrutiny (sections 143(2) and 142(1))The return has been picked for detailed assessment, and the officer wants explanations and documents.
Reassessment (sections 148A and 148)The department believes some income escaped tax in an earlier year.
Penalty (section 270A)Penalty for under-reported or misreported income, usually after an assessment adds income.
Demand and recoveryOutstanding demand, adjustment against refunds, or recovery action.

These are the 1961 Act section numbers, which still apply to FY 2025-26 and earlier years. Notices for tax year 2026-27 onwards will cite the new sections of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

How we handle a notice

  1. Read it the same day: the section, the year, the question asked and the deadline.
  2. Reconcile: your return against AIS, Form 26AS, bank statements and books, to find the real reason behind the notice.
  3. Draft the reply with a clear explanation, a working and supporting documents, in the order the officer asked.
  4. File on the e-filing portal before the date, or ask for more time in writing if documents are pending.
  5. Hearing: we ask for a video hearing where the amount is significant, and attend it with you.

Appeals

  • Commissioner (Appeals): filed within 30 days of the assessment or penalty order.
  • Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT): filed within two months of the Commissioner (Appeals) order.
  • Stay of demand: while the appeal is pending, we apply for a stay, which is usually granted on payment of a part of the demand.

Appeals and hearings are handled with Adv. Prabhash Mishra, our mentor and a tax advocate with more than 15 years in direct tax, indirect tax and corporate law, and a member of the Delhi Bar Association.

Frequently asked questions

I got a notice and the deadline is close. What should I do first?

Send us the notice and your return for that year straight away. If the time is too short to reply fully, we file a request for more time before the deadline, so the officer does not pass an order without hearing you.

Do I have to visit the income tax office?

Usually not. Most assessments are faceless, so replies are filed online and hearings, if any, are by video call.

The demand is wrong. Do I still have to pay it?

If you file an appeal, you can apply for a stay of the demand while the appeal is pending. A stay is commonly granted on payment of a part of the disputed demand, and the rest waits for the appeal.

Can you take over a case that another consultant started?

Yes. Share the notices, replies already filed and any orders, and we will review where the case stands before the next step.
