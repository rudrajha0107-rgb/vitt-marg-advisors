Germany tax questions Indian businesses run into

Cross-borderSeptember 2026Vitt-Marg Advisors

Germany is one of India's largest trade partners, and most Indian companies meet the German tax system before they ever set up a company there — through an engineer posted on site, an Amazon warehouse, or a client that suddenly withholds tax on an invoice.

The first question: do you have a permanent establishment?

Germany taxes a non-resident company only on German-source business profits, which usually means real activity on the ground. A permanent establishment (PE) under the India-Germany tax treaty can arise from a fixed place of business, a long-running installation or construction site, or an agent in Germany who habitually concludes contracts for you.

This matters because a PE changes everything: German corporate tax returns, profit attribution, payroll for people working there, and a transfer-pricing file. Teams deputed to a client site for months at a time are the usual trigger, and the discovery usually comes late.

If a German company is your customer

  • Withholding tax. Germany withholds on royalties and certain licence payments. The India-Germany treaty reduces the rate, but relief generally has to be applied for in advance through the Federal Central Tax Office, with a residency certificate — otherwise you pay the full rate and claim a refund later.
  • VAT on services. For most B2B services to a German business customer, the reverse charge applies: the customer accounts for the VAT and you don't need a German VAT registration. Get their VAT ID and put the reverse-charge reference on the invoice.
  • Goods and e-commerce are different. Stock held in a German warehouse, or B2C sales, generally do require a German VAT registration. VAT is 19% standard, 7% reduced.

If you set up a German entity

  • GmbH — share capital of €25,000, notarised articles, entry in the commercial register, trade office registration, and a tax number from the Finanzamt.
  • UG (haftungsbeschränkt) — the "mini-GmbH", which can start with as little as €1 but must retain part of its profits each year until reserves reach €25,000, at which point it converts.
  • Tax: corporate income tax of 15%, a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that, and municipal trade tax that varies by city — an effective burden of roughly 30%. Filing is electronic, through the ELSTER system.

The India side

  • ODI or LRS for the investment, with Form FC, the UIN, and an annual APR — the same framework as any other overseas investment.
  • Schedule FA in your ITR for the shareholding and any German bank account.
  • Form 67 to claim credit for German tax against Indian tax on the same income.
  • Transfer pricing for services, management charges or goods between the Indian and German entities.
  • 15CA/15CB on remittances out of India, with the treaty rate applied correctly.

The most expensive Germany mistakes we see aren't tax computations. They're a deputation arrangement that quietly created a PE, and a royalty invoice raised without advance treaty relief.
