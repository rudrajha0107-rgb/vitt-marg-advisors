Cost audit penalty: what it costs to skip cost records, the cost auditor or CRA-4

Cost AuditNovember 2026Vitt-Marg Advisors

Cost audit defaults tend to surface years later, when the Registrar of Companies matches turnover in the financial statements with the CRA forms on record. By then the fine is the smaller problem. The bigger problem is reconstructing several years of cost records under deadline. Here is what the law provides today and what is being proposed.

The short version. Any default under Section 148 makes the company liable to a fine of ₹25,000 to ₹5 lakh, and every officer in default to a fine of ₹10,000 to ₹1 lakh. A cost auditor in default faces ₹25,000 to ₹5 lakh, or four times the audit fee if that is lower. Late CRA forms also attract additional filing fees.

Which defaults are covered

  • Not maintaining cost records in Form CRA-1 when they are required.
  • Not appointing a cost auditor within 180 days from the start of the financial year, or not filing Form CRA-2.
  • Not getting the cost audit report in Form CRA-3.
  • Not filing the cost audit report with the MCA in Form CRA-4 within 30 days of receiving it, with the Board's explanations.

Penalties under the current law

Section 148(8) of the Companies Act, 2013 links every default under Section 148 to the penalties in Section 147.

WhoPenalty under Section 147
The companyFine of not less than ₹25,000, which may extend to ₹5 lakh
Every officer in defaultFine of not less than ₹10,000, which may extend to ₹1 lakh
The cost auditorFine of ₹25,000 to ₹5 lakh, or four times the remuneration, whichever is less
The cost auditor, if the default is knowing or wilfulImprisonment up to one year and a fine of ₹50,000 to ₹25 lakh, or eight times the remuneration, whichever is less

The one-year imprisonment that once applied to the company's officers was removed by the Companies (Amendment) Act, 2020. A cost auditor found guilty may also have to refund the fee and pay damages for loss caused by a misleading report.

The filing fee side

A late CRA-2 or CRA-4 attracts an additional fee under the Companies (Registration Offices and Fees) Rules, 2014, which grows with the length of the delay. For FY 2025-26 there is relief: CRA-4 filed by 31 December 2026 carries no additional fee. Our note on the CRA-4 relief explains it.

What the Corporate Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026 proposes

The Bill was introduced in the Lok Sabha in March 2026 and referred to a Joint Parliamentary Committee. Until it is passed and notified, the current law above applies. As proposed, it would make cost compliance a more personal responsibility.

  • Named officers. The managing director, whole-time director in charge of finance, CFO or person charged by the Board with keeping cost records would face a penalty of ₹5 lakh in a listed company and ₹50,000 in any other company for not maintaining them.
  • Procedural defaults. For failures on appointing the cost auditor or furnishing information, the company would face ₹10,000 plus ₹100 a day, up to ₹2 lakh, and each officer in default ₹10,000 plus ₹100 a day, up to ₹50,000.
  • Cost Accounting Standards would be recognised by the Central Government, and firms of cost accountants would be expressly allowed to act as cost auditors.

If you have already missed a year

  1. Appoint a cost auditor now and file CRA-2, even if late.
  2. Rebuild the cost records from the books, stock registers and production data for the missed years.
  3. Complete the audit and file CRA-4 with the additional fee, or within the relief window where it applies.
  4. If the Registrar has already issued a notice, reply with the corrective steps taken. Voluntary correction usually leads to a far lower penalty than a default discovered by the department.

Frequently asked questions

What is the penalty for not conducting a cost audit?

Under Section 148(8) read with Section 147(1), the company can be fined ₹25,000 to ₹5 lakh and every officer in default ₹10,000 to ₹1 lakh.

Is there imprisonment for cost audit default?

Not for the company's officers since the Companies (Amendment) Act, 2020. A cost auditor who contravenes the law knowingly or wilfully can face imprisonment of up to one year and a higher fine.

What is the penalty on a cost auditor?

A fine of ₹25,000 to ₹5 lakh or four times the remuneration, whichever is less. For a knowing or wilful default, up to one year of imprisonment and a fine of ₹50,000 to ₹25 lakh or eight times the remuneration, whichever is less.

Has the Corporate Laws (Amendment) Bill 2026 changed cost audit penalties?

The Bill proposes new personal penalties on directors and CFOs for not maintaining cost records, but these apply only once the Bill is passed and notified. Until then the current Section 147 penalties apply.
