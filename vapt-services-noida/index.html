Who needs VAPT

  • Regulated businesses: companies dealing with banks, NBFCs, insurers, brokers and payment systems are often required by their regulator or partner to test their systems regularly.
  • Vendors to large companies: enterprise clients increasingly ask for a recent VAPT report before they share data or sign a contract.
  • Companies preparing for ISO 27001 or the DPDP Act: testing is a practical way to show that your security safeguards actually work.
  • Any business with a customer-facing app or portal: before launch, after major changes, and at least once a year.

What you receive

  • A risk-ranked report: each finding rated critical, high, medium or low, with proof and the business impact explained in plain language.
  • Fix guidance: specific steps for your developers or IT vendor, not a generic checklist.
  • A retest after you fix the findings, and an updated report showing what is closed.
  • A management summary you can share with your board, bank or client.

How we work

  1. Scoping call: what is in scope, test windows and who to contact if something breaks.

  3. Testing: automated scanning plus manual testing by experienced testers, usually 1 to 3 weeks depending on scope.
  4. Report, fixes and retest.

Frequently asked questions

What is VAPT?

VAPT stands for Vulnerability Assessment and Penetration Testing. The assessment finds known weaknesses in your systems, and the penetration test tries to exploit them the way an attacker would, to show which ones are real risks.

How often should we do VAPT?

At least once a year, and also after a major release, a new app or portal, or a significant change in your network or cloud setup. Some regulators and clients ask for it every six months.

Will testing disrupt our website or app?

Testing is planned to avoid disruption. We agree test windows in advance, avoid destructive tests on live systems unless you approve them, and keep a contact person available throughout.

Do you provide a retest?

Yes. After your team fixes the findings, we retest them and issue an updated report showing which issues are closed.

Is your VAPT report accepted by banks and clients?

Our reports follow the format banks and enterprise clients usually ask for. If your regulator or client requires a CERT-In empanelled auditor specifically, tell us at the start so we can plan for that requirement.
