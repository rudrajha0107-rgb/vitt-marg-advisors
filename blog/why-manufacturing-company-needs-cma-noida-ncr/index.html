Running a manufacturing company in Noida or NCR? Why you need a CMA

Cost AuditOctober 2026Vitt-Marg Advisors

Most manufacturing companies in Noida and across Delhi NCR know their monthly sales and their bank balance. Far fewer can say what one unit of each product actually costs to make, which customers make them money, or why margins fell while sales grew. That gap is what a Cost and Management Accountant (CMA) is trained to close. In some cases the law requires a CMA. In most, it is simply a good business decision. This note covers both.

Who a CMA is, and how that differs from a CA

A CMA is a member of the Institute of Cost Accountants of India (ICMAI), the statutory body set up under the Cost and Works Accountants Act, 1959. The training centres on cost: how costs build up in a factory, how they should be assigned to products, and how they can be brought down.

Main focusFinancial reporting, statutory audit, income taxProduct costing, pricing, efficiency, cost control
Answers the questionAre the accounts true and fair? Is the tax right?What does each product cost, and where is money being lost?
Exclusive by lawStatutory audit, tax auditCost audit under Section 148

It isn't one or the other. A well-run factory needs its accounts audited and its costs understood. They are different jobs.

Where the law requires a CMA

  • Cost audit: under Section 148 of the Companies Act, 2013, only a Cost Accountant in practice (or a firm of them) can be appointed as cost auditor. It applies to companies in listed sectors above set turnover limits: ₹50 crore overall and ₹25 crore for the product in regulated sectors such as pharma, telecom, power and fertilisers, and ₹100 crore overall and ₹35 crore for the product in non-regulated sectors such as steel, automobiles, chemicals and machinery. Your statutory auditor cannot be your cost auditor.
  • Cost records: companies in the listed sectors with turnover of ₹35 crore or more must maintain cost records in Form CRA-1, even if a cost audit doesn't apply. Setting up records that will stand up to a later audit is CMA work.
  • Internal audit: under Section 138, the internal auditor can be a Cost Accountant, a Chartered Accountant or another professional chosen by the Board. For a factory, a cost-trained internal auditor tests the areas where money actually leaks: stores, scrap, job work and production records.
  • GST representation: a CMA can appear for a business before the GST authorities as an authorised representative.

Why NCR manufacturers in particular

Delhi NCR is one of India's largest manufacturing belts. Noida and Greater Noida are a centre for mobile phones and electronics, and the UP government says the state makes more than half of India's mobile phones. Gurugram and Manesar have the auto-component cluster, Faridabad has engineering and machinery, and Ghaziabad and Sahibabad have steel, chemicals and fabrication. Garments and handicraft exporters work out of Noida's industrial sectors.

What these businesses share is thin margins, many products, a lot of job work, and big banks behind them. In that setting, a 2% error in costing can wipe out most of the year's profit without anyone noticing until the balance sheet is drawn up.

The problem a CMA usually finds first

Most small and mid-sized factories spread overheads (power, machine depreciation, supervision, factory rent) across products in proportion to sales. That is easy, and often wrong. Here is a simplified example of a Noida unit making two products, with ₹3 crore of factory overheads.

The product the owner thought was the better one is losing money on every unit. Pushing more of it, or cutting its price to win volume, makes things worse. This is the most common finding in a first costing exercise, and it changes pricing, product mix and sales targets.

What a CMA does for a manufacturing company

1. Product costing and pricing

Builds a cost sheet for each product: material, labour, power, machine time, overheads and packing, based on how the factory actually works. Quotes and price revisions are then based on cost, not on what competitors charge.

2. Margin by product and by customer

Shows which products and which customers make money once discounts, freight, credit period and rejections are counted. Large buyers who pay late and return goods often turn out to be the least profitable.

3. Material yield, scrap and wastage

Sets norms for how much raw material each unit should use, then tracks the actual. For a steel fabricator, a plastics moulder or a garment unit, a few percent of extra wastage on material that is 60% of cost is a large number. Unrecorded scrap sales are also a common leak.

4. Standard costing and variance analysis

Sets a standard cost and explains every month why actual cost differed: material price, material usage, labour efficiency, or idle machines. Management gets a short report that points to the cause, not just the total.

5. Inventory valuation and stock control

Values raw material, work in progress and finished goods correctly under the accounting standards. Wrong stock values distort profit and tax. Finds slow-moving and obsolete stock before it becomes a write-off, and reconciles stock with job workers.

6. Make-or-buy and job-work decisions

Noida and Ghaziabad have a deep job-work ecosystem. Whether to make a component in-house or send it out depends on the real cost of your own machine hours. A CMA works that out on facts, not habit.

7. Capacity, break-even and expansion

Works out the break-even volume, the cost of idle capacity and whether a new machine or shift will actually pay back. Expansions planned on sales targets alone are a common reason factories run short of cash.

8. Budgets and working capital

Builds the annual budget and the cash-flow plan, and tracks the working-capital cycle: stock days, debtor days and creditor days. Most factory cash problems come from this cycle, not from losses.

9. Bank finance: CMA data and project reports

Banks want projected financials, the "CMA data" (Credit Monitoring Arrangement) format, before sanctioning or renewing cash-credit limits and term loans. Projections built on real product costs and capacity are more credible to the bank and easier to defend at renewal. Stock statements for drawing power also depend on proper stock valuation.

10. GST and compliance with a cost view

Checks input tax credit on inputs and capital goods, goods sent for job work and the related ITC-04 reporting, and how GST rate changes affect pricing. Cost records also support the company if the department questions valuation.

Signs your factory needs a CMA now

  • Sales are growing but profit isn't.
  • You can't say the cost of one unit of each product without guessing.
  • Prices are set by matching competitors or adding a fixed markup on material.
  • Physical stock and book stock don't agree, or there are big adjustments at year-end.
  • Scrap and wastage aren't measured against any norm.
  • The bank keeps asking questions about your projections or stock statements.
  • Your turnover has crossed ₹35 crore and no one has checked whether cost records or cost audit apply.

Full-time or outside CMA?

A large plant may have a CMA on staff and still needs an independent cost auditor where Section 148 applies. For most MSMEs in Noida and NCR, an outside CMA on a monthly or quarterly retainer works better: the costing system is set up once, then reviewed each month with a short MIS on margins, variances and stock. The cost is usually recovered from the first pricing correction or the first scrap leak found.
