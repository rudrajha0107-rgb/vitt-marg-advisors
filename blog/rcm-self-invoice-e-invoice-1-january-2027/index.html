E-invoice for RCM self-invoices from 1 January 2027: RCMDOM, RCMIMP and what to set up now

GSTNovember 2026Vitt-Marg Advisors

Until now, e-invoicing covered only the invoices a business issues to its customers. From 1 January 2027, it also covers two kinds of invoices a business issues to itself under reverse charge. If your turnover is above ₹5 crore, your purchase team now becomes part of e-invoicing.

In one line: from 1 January 2027, a business with aggregate turnover above ₹5 crore must generate an IRN for every reverse charge self-invoice it raises for purchases from unregistered suppliers and for imported services. The portal opens for live use on 15 December 2026.

What exactly is covered

New document typeWhat it isTypical examples
RCMDOMSelf-invoice for goods or services bought from an unregistered person in India, where you pay GST under reverse chargeScrap from a kabadiwala, an unregistered landlord where RCM applies, an unregistered transporter or security agency
RCMIMPSelf-invoice for services imported from outside IndiaForeign software subscriptions, overseas consultants, foreign advertising platforms

Import of goods is not covered. It continues through the bill of entry at customs.

The legal basis is Notification No. 04/2026-Central Tax dated 26 October 2026, which adds these two document types to the e-invoicing notification, and Notification No. 05/2026-Central Tax, which updates the e-invoice schema to version 1.2. GSTN explained the rollout in Advisory No. 688.

The dates

DateWhat happens

Who is covered

The same businesses that already issue e-invoices: those whose aggregate turnover crossed ₹5 crore in any financial year from 2017-18 onwards. If you are below the threshold, you continue to raise self-invoices without an IRN.

Why it matters for your input tax credit

For a business required to e-invoice, an invoice without a valid IRN is not treated as a proper invoice under the CGST Rules. A self-invoice raised from January 2027 without an IRN therefore puts the input tax credit on that reverse charge payment at risk, even though you paid the tax yourself.

Two 30-day clocks to keep in mind

  1. Raising the self-invoice: under Rule 47A, the self-invoice must be issued within 30 days of receiving the goods or services. This has applied to every registered business since 1 November 2024.
  2. Reporting to the IRP: businesses with turnover of ₹10 crore and above cannot report an invoice to the IRP more than 30 days after its date. GSTN has not yet said whether this limit will apply to RCMDOM and RCMIMP, so plan as if it does.

The safe practice is to raise the self-invoice and generate the IRN within a week of receiving the goods or the foreign service invoice, not at month-end.

Checklist for December

  1. List your reverse charge purchases. Unregistered suppliers, such as scrap sellers, transporters, security agencies and landlords, and every foreign service you pay for, including software and advertising.
  2. Ask your software provider whether it supports schema 1.2 with RCMDOM and RCMIMP, and by when. Test in the sandbox.
  3. Move the trigger for the self-invoice to the goods receipt or the payment approval.
  4. Train the purchase and accounts team, since they will now generate IRNs, not only the sales team.
  5. Test with real invoices between 15 and 31 December, when use is voluntary.
  6. Keep December and January documents separate, because supplies before 1 January 2027 stay under the old rules.

Frequently asked questions

From when is e-invoicing mandatory for RCM self-invoices?

From 1 January 2027, under Notification No. 04/2026-Central Tax dated 26 October 2026. Live IRN generation opens on 15 December 2026, with voluntary use until 31 December.

What are RCMDOM and RCMIMP?

RCMDOM is the self-invoice for purchases from unregistered suppliers in India under reverse charge. RCMIMP is the self-invoice for import of services.

Who must e-invoice RCM self-invoices?

Businesses whose aggregate turnover exceeded ₹5 crore in any financial year from 2017-18 onwards, the same group already covered by e-invoicing for sales.

Is import of goods covered?

No. Import of goods continues through the bill of entry at customs and is not part of this change.

What if I raise a self-invoice without an IRN after 1 January 2027?

For a business required to e-invoice, an invoice without a valid IRN is not treated as a proper invoice, which puts the input tax credit on that payment at risk.
