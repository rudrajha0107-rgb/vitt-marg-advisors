Setting up a Saudi company from India: what it actually involves

Cross-borderSeptember 2026Vitt-Marg Advisors

Saudi Arabia has become one of the most active markets for Indian businesses — IT services, contracting, logistics, manufacturing and trading. Under the Kingdom's reformed investment law, most sectors now allow 100% foreign ownership without a Saudi partner. The paperwork, though, runs on two tracks at once: approvals in Riyadh and FEMA compliance in India. Getting one right and the other wrong is the usual reason projects stall.

The Saudi side, in sequence

  1. Pick the activity first. Everything follows from the exact economic activity you register. Ownership limits, capital expectations, sector approvals and Saudization targets all attach to the activity, not to the company.
  2. Register with MISA (the Ministry of Investment). The old licensing process has been replaced with an investment registration system, but it is still the gateway — without it you cannot incorporate, get a commercial registration, open a bank account or sponsor visas.
  3. Attested parent-company documents. This is where most Indian applicants get stuck. MISA generally expects an established business, not a company incorporated last month for the application: commercial registration and audited financial statements of the Indian parent, attested and legalised through the Saudi Embassy. Names, addresses and shareholding must reconcile exactly across every document.
  4. Commercial Registration (CR) from the Ministry of Commerce, articles of association, and the National Address.
  5. Register with the authorities: ZATCA for VAT and corporate tax, GOSI for social insurance, and Qiwa for workforce and Saudization.
  6. Bank account, general manager's Iqama, and visas.

Structure choice matters early. An LLC is a separate Saudi company; a branch of your Indian company carries the parent's liability and is taxed differently; a representative office can't trade at all. Changing your mind later means re-doing approvals.

What you'll live with afterwards

  • VAT at 15%, with e-invoicing through the ZATCA system.
  • Corporate tax on the foreign-owned share of profits, alongside Zakat for the Saudi-owned share.
  • Withholding tax on payments out of Saudi Arabia — which is exactly where the India-Saudi tax treaty matters, especially for management fees and royalties charged by the Indian parent.
  • Saudization quotas under Nitaqat, tied to your activity and headcount. This is an operating cost, not a formality.

The India side that people forget

Investing in a foreign company is regulated under FEMA and the Overseas Investment rules:

  • Route: an Indian company invests through the ODI route via its AD bank, which files the Form FC and obtains a UIN for the foreign entity. A resident individual invests within the LRS limit of USD 250,000 per financial year, subject to the conditions in the Overseas Investment Rules.
  • Every remittance for share capital or loans goes through the AD bank against that UIN, with 15CA/15CB where applicable.
  • Annual Performance Report (APR) for the foreign entity, due each year — miss it and further remittances get blocked.
  • Schedule FA in your Indian income-tax return once you hold shares abroad. Non-disclosure is penalised under the Black Money Act, and the penalty far exceeds the tax involved.
  • Transfer pricing the moment the Indian company and the Saudi company transact — services, management fees, or goods.
