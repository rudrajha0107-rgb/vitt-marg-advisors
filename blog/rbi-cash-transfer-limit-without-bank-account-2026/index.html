How much cash can you send without a bank account? RBI's 2026 money transfer rules explained

Personal FinanceNovember 2026Vitt-Marg Advisors

On 11 November 2026, the Reserve Bank of India issued the Domestic Money Transfer Directions, 2026. They bring all the older circulars on sending money within India into one set of rules. Many headlines made it sound like new limits. In fact, the cash limits stay the same. What has changed is the paperwork and the checks.

In one line: without a bank account, you can send up to ₹50,000 in cash at a bank branch through NEFT, or ₹5,000 per transfer and ₹25,000 a month through a banking agent or ATM. Someone without a bank account can receive up to ₹10,000 per transfer and ₹25,000 a month in cash.

The limits

SituationLimit
You walk into a bank branch with cash and no account there, and send money to someone's account by NEFTUp to ₹50,000
You send cash through a business correspondent (banking agent) or an ATM₹5,000 per transfer, ₹25,000 per month
Money is sent from a bank account to someone who has no bank account, and they collect it in cash₹10,000 per transfer, ₹25,000 per month for each receiver

What is new

  • Verified mobile number and ID. If you send cash without an account, you must give a verified mobile number and a self-attested officially valid document, such as Aadhaar or a voter ID.
  • Extra authentication on every transaction, usually an OTP.
  • Cash transfers are flagged. The sender's details travel with the NEFT or IMPS message, and cash-based transfers carry a separate identifier, so the receiving bank knows the money came in as cash.
  • Banks must watch for patterns. A sudden rise in credits to an account, or to a group of accounts, must be checked and, where needed, reported as suspicious.
  • Alerts to the receiver when money is credited.
  • Card-to-card transfers are outside these rules and follow their own.

Who these rules are for

Mainly migrant workers and families who send money home in cash, small shopkeepers who pay suppliers in cash, and anyone without a bank account in the place they work. The rules apply to commercial banks, regional rural banks, co-operative banks and authorised payment operators.

The tax side

Sending cash through these channels is legal within the limits, but cash still matters under income tax.

  • Receiving ₹2 lakh or more in cash from one person in a day, or for one transaction, is prohibited, and the penalty equals the amount received. Read our note on the ₹2 lakh cash limit.
  • Large cash deposits are reported to the tax department through the bank's annual statement and show up in your AIS.
  • For a business, cash expenses above ₹10,000 paid to one person in a day are not allowed as a deduction.

Because these transfers are now clearly tagged as cash, frequent cash remittances into one account are easier for banks and the tax department to spot. If you regularly receive money this way, keep a simple record of who sent it and why.

Frequently asked questions

How much cash can I send without a bank account in 2026?

Up to ₹50,000 through NEFT at a bank branch, or ₹5,000 per transfer and ₹25,000 a month through a business correspondent or ATM, under RBI's Domestic Money Transfer Directions, 2026.

Did RBI change the cash transfer limits in November 2026?

No. The limits stay the same. The new directions consolidate older rules and add checks such as a verified mobile number, ID, extra authentication and tagging of cash-based transfers.

How much cash can a person without a bank account receive?

Up to ₹10,000 per transfer and ₹25,000 a month for each receiver, collected through an ATM, agent or other authorised channel.

Do card-to-card transfers come under these rules?

No. Card-to-card transfers are excluded and follow their own rules.
