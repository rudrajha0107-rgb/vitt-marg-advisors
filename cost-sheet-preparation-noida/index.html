What a cost sheet shows

A cost sheet builds the cost of a product in layers. Each layer answers a different business question, which is why it is more useful than a single total.

LayerWhat goes inThe question it answers
Prime costDirect materials, direct labour and direct expenses such as job-work chargesWhat does the product itself consume?
Works costPrime cost plus factory overheads: power, fuel, repairs, supervision, factory rent and depreciation of machinesWhat does it cost to make inside the factory?
Cost of productionWorks cost plus administrative overheads related to production, adjusted for work in progressWhat is the value of a finished unit in stock?
Cost of salesCost of goods sold plus selling and distribution overheads: freight out, commission, marketingWhat does it cost to make and sell?
Profit and priceSelling price minus cost of salesHow much does each unit really earn?

Frequently asked questions

Is a cost sheet compulsory by law?

Not for every business. Companies covered by the cost records rules must keep cost records, and a cost sheet is the core of those records. For everyone else it is a management tool, and usually the fastest way to find out which products make money.

What is a CAS-4 certificate and when do I need one?

It is a certificate of cost of production prepared under Cost Accounting Standard 4. Under GST valuation rules, goods supplied to a related party or used captively are often valued at 110% of the cost of production, and the certificate supports that value if the department asks.

We already use Tally. Is that not enough?

Tally gives you totals by account head, not cost per unit. A cost sheet takes those totals, adds production data such as quantities and machine hours, and turns them into a cost for each product.

How often should a cost sheet be updated?

Monthly is ideal for businesses where raw material prices move often, such as steel, plastics and chemicals. Quarterly works for steadier businesses. At minimum, update it whenever a major input price changes.
