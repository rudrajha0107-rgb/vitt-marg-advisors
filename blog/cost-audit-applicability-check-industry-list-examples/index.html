Is cost audit applicable to your company? A three-step check with worked examples

Cost AuditNovember 2026Vitt-Marg Advisors

Cost audit is one of those requirements that companies often discover late, usually when a bank, a buyer or the statutory auditor asks for the cost audit report. The good news is that the test is mechanical. Three questions, answered in order, tell you whether your company must keep cost records, and whether it also needs a cost audit.

The short version. Cost records are needed if your product or service is in the notified list and your total turnover last year was ₹35 crore or more. A cost audit is needed on top of that if turnover crossed ₹50 crore (with ₹25 crore from covered products) in regulated sectors, or ₹100 crore (with ₹35 crore from covered products) in other listed sectors.

Step 1: Is your product or service in the notified list?

Rule 3 of the Companies (Cost Records and Audit) Rules, 2014 lists the products and services covered, mostly by their customs tariff heading. The list has two tables.

Table A: regulated sectorsTable B: other sectors (examples)
Telecommunication servicesIron and steel, aluminium, copper and other base metals
Electricity generation, transmission and supplyCement, ores and mineral products, coal
Petroleum productsMachinery, electrical and electronic equipment, medical devices
Drugs and pharmaceuticalsMotor vehicles, tyres and tubes, railway rolling stock
FertilisersChemicals, plastics and polymers, insecticides, cosmetics
Sugar and industrial alcoholTextiles, jute, paper, glass, rubber, edible oil, milk powder, tea, coffee
Health services (hospitals, diagnostic centres), education services, roads and infrastructure projects, port and aeronautical services

If none of your products or services appears in either table, the rules do not apply to you at all. If even one product appears, move to step 2. Always check against the exact tariff heading, because two similar-sounding products can fall under different headings.

Step 2: Do you need to keep cost records?

A company making a listed product or providing a listed service must maintain cost records in Form CRA-1 if its overall turnover from all products and services in the preceding financial year was ₹35 crore or more. Micro and small enterprises are outside this requirement.

Step 3: Do you also need a cost audit?

SectorOverall turnover (preceding year)Turnover of covered products
Table A: regulated sectors₹50 crore or more₹25 crore or more
Table B: other sectors₹100 crore or more₹35 crore or more

Both conditions in the row must be met. A company can therefore need cost records but not a cost audit, which is a common position for mid-sized manufacturers.

Who is exempt from cost audit

  • A company whose export revenue in foreign exchange is more than 75% of its total revenue.
  • A company operating from a Special Economic Zone.
  • A company generating electricity only for captive consumption through a captive generating plant.

These exemptions are from the audit. Check separately whether cost records must still be maintained.

Timing: when the requirement starts

The test always uses the preceding year's figures. If your FY 2025-26 turnover crossed the limit, cost records and, where applicable, the cost audit apply for FY 2026-27. The cost auditor must then be appointed within 180 days from the start of that year, and Form CRA-2 filed. Our note on appointing a cost auditor walks through that step.

Frequently asked questions

What is the turnover limit for maintaining cost records?

A company in a notified industry must maintain cost records if its overall turnover from all products and services in the preceding financial year was ₹35 crore or more.

What is the turnover limit for cost audit?

For regulated sectors, overall turnover of ₹50 crore or more and ₹25 crore or more from covered products. For other notified sectors, overall turnover of ₹100 crore or more and ₹35 crore or more from covered products.

Is a company in an SEZ required to get a cost audit?

No. A company operating from a Special Economic Zone is exempt from cost audit under Rule 4(3) of the Companies (Cost Records and Audit) Rules, 2014.

Do micro and small enterprises need cost records?

No. The requirement to maintain cost records does not apply to companies classified as micro or small enterprises.

Which year's turnover is used to decide applicability?

The turnover of the immediately preceding financial year. If the limits are crossed in FY 2025-26, the requirement applies for FY 2026-27.
