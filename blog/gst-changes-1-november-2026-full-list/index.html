GST changes from 1 November 2026: the full list for businesses

GSTNovember 2026Vitt-Marg Advisors

CBIC notified a large set of GST changes on 27 October 2026, almost all effective from 1 November 2026. They came on the recommendations of the GST Council and touch hotels, restaurants, e-commerce sellers, transporters, factories and traders. Here is everything in one place.

What "same-service credit" means: a hotel can now take input tax credit only on rooms or services it buys from another supplier of the same service, for example when it books rooms from another hotel for its guests. For hotels, outdoor catering and electric vehicles, that credit is capped at 2.5% central tax, even if the other supplier charged more. Credit on other purchases, such as furniture or maintenance, is still not allowed at the 5% rate.

New exemptions

  • Storage and warehousing of seeds for sowing.
  • Curing of coffee for coffee growers.
  • Services of the Seamen's Provident Fund Organisation to its members.
  • Grant of toll collection rights by the government or a government entity.
  • Helicopter seat-share travel to and from the North-Eastern States, Sikkim and Bagdogra.

Scrap: reverse charge, registration and TDS

Plastic, tyre and e-waste scrap and used cooking oil bought from unregistered sellers now come under reverse charge. Every seller of these goods and of metal scrap must register, and registered buyers deduct 2% GST TDS on purchases above ₹2.5 lakh. This is the change that affects most factories, so we have covered it separately: GST on scrap from 1 November 2026.

Post-supply discounts are now simpler

Sections 153 to 155 of the Finance Act, 2026 came into force on 1 November 2026 through Notification No. 03/2026-Central Tax. They rewrite section 15(3)(b) of the CGST Act. A discount given after the sale, such as a year-end volume discount, can now be reduced from the taxable value if the supplier issues a credit note and the buyer reverses the matching input tax credit. The same law also widens provisional refunds to cover refunds of accumulated credit under the inverted duty structure.

This is good news for manufacturers and distributors who give trade schemes, but the buyer's credit reversal now carries the weight. Make sure your dealers actually reverse the credit, because the discount depends on it.

Coming next

  • 1 December 2026 and 1 January 2027: parts of the new registration rules, including a virtual principal place of business for e-commerce sellers, take effect in stages.
  • 1 January 2027: e-invoicing extends to reverse charge self-invoices for businesses above ₹5 crore turnover. See our note on RCM e-invoicing.

Invoices that fall across 1 November

Where a supply, its invoice and its payment fall on different sides of 1 November, section 14 of the CGST Act decides the rate: generally, the rate follows the two events that fall on the same side of the change. For example, retreaded tractor tyres supplied and invoiced in October but paid for in November stay at 18%. Check large transactions individually.

Frequently asked questions

Can hotels claim input tax credit from 1 November 2026?

Hotels with room tariffs up to ₹7,500 a day still pay 5%, but can now claim credit on the same service bought from another supplier, capped at 2.5% central tax. Credit on other purchases is still not allowed at the 5% rate.

What is the GST rate on retreaded tractor tyres from 1 November 2026?

Five percent, down from 18%, under Notification No. 02/2026-Central Tax (Rate).

Who pays GST on e-commerce delivery from 1 November 2026?

The e-commerce operator pays 5% without input tax credit on delivery services by suppliers who are not required to register, other than postal and courier services.

What changed for post-supply discounts?

From 1 November 2026, a discount given after the supply can be reduced from the taxable value if the supplier issues a credit note and the buyer reverses the matching input tax credit, under the amended section 15(3)(b).
