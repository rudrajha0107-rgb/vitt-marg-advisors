Statutory audit: who needs it

  • Every company, whatever its size or turnover, must have its accounts audited under the Companies Act, 2013. This includes private limited companies, one person companies and Section 8 companies, even with no business in the year.
  • LLPs need an audit when turnover is more than ₹40 lakh or partners' contribution is more than ₹25 lakh in the year.
  • Trusts and societies need audits under their own laws and, for tax exemption, under the Income-tax Act.

Along with the audit report, many companies need a CARO 2020 report. Small companies, one person companies and some small private companies are exempt from it. Reporting on internal financial controls is also needed, except for private companies that meet the turnover or borrowing exemption. We check both for you at the start of the audit, not at the end.

Tax audit: who needs it

  • Business turnover above ₹1 crore, or above ₹10 crore where cash receipts and cash payments are each 5% or less of the total.
  • Professional receipts above ₹50 lakh.
  • Presumptive cases that declare income below the presumptive rate while total income is above the basic exemption limit.

For FY 2025-26, the tax audit falls under section 44AB of the 1961 Act, in Forms 3CA or 3CB with Form 3CD. CBDT extended the report date to 21 October 2026. From tax year 2026-27, the audit moves to section 63 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, and the three forms are combined into a single Form 26. Read our note on tax audit due dates.

Frequently asked questions

Our company had no business this year. Does it still need an audit?

Yes. Every company needs an audit of its accounts, even with nil turnover. Without it, the company cannot file AOC-4 and MGT-7, and late fees on those forms keep adding up.

Can you take over from our current auditor mid-term?

Yes, if the outgoing auditor resigns or is removed with the required process. We first write to the previous auditor, as professional rules require, and then file the forms for the change.

Is a tax audit needed if our turnover is ₹1.5 crore but almost everything is through the bank?

If cash receipts and cash payments are each 5% or less of the total, the tax audit limit is ₹10 crore, so you may not need one. We check the cash ratio from your books before you decide.

What is Form 26?

It is the new tax audit report under the Income-tax Act, 2025. It replaces Forms 3CA, 3CB and 3CD, and will be used first for tax year 2026-27, with reports due in 2027.
