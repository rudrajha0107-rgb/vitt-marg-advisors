Buyer not paying within 45 days? Your rights under the MSME law, and what the 2026 Amendment Act changes

MSMENovember 2026Vitt-Marg Advisors

If you run a small factory or service business in Noida and a large customer keeps you waiting for payment, the law gives you real power. A buyer must pay a registered micro or small enterprise within 45 days. If the buyer pays late, it owes compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate, and it cannot contract out of this. In 2026 Parliament also amended the MSME law, and two of its changes take effect between October and December 2026.

In one line: written credit terms can be at most 45 days; with no written terms the limit is 15 days. After that, interest runs every month, the buyer loses its income tax deduction until it pays, and you can file a claim online that the buyer can only challenge after depositing 75% of the award.

Who is protected

The protection under sections 15 to 23 of the MSMED Act, 2006 is for micro and small enterprises with a valid Udyam registration on the date of supply. Medium enterprises are not covered. The supplier, not the buyer, must be micro or small; the buyer can be of any size, including a large company or a government undertaking.

When the payment is due

SituationPayment due by
Written agreement with credit of 45 days or lessThe agreed date
Written agreement with credit of more than 45 days45 days from acceptance of goods or services
No written agreement15 days from acceptance

Acceptance usually means the day of delivery. If the buyer raises a written objection within 15 days, the clock starts when the defect is fixed. If it raises no objection, the goods are treated as accepted.

The interest the buyer owes

Under section 16, interest runs from the due date until the day of payment, at three times the bank rate notified by RBI, compounded with monthly rests. A clause in the purchase order that says otherwise does not apply.

Example. A supplier is owed ₹5,00,000 and the buyer pays three months late. If the bank rate is 6.5%, the interest rate is 19.5% a year, or 1.625% a month. Compounded for three months, the interest comes to about ₹24,773. The bank rate changes from time to time, so check the current figure before you calculate.

The tax hit for buyers

From tax year 2026-27, the new Income-tax Act, 2025 continues the old section 43B(h) rule in section 37(2)(g). A buyer can claim its purchase or expense from a micro or small supplier only in the year it actually pays, if it pays after the MSME time limit. A bill of March 2027 paid in May 2027 is therefore disallowed for 2026-27 and allowed only in 2027-28. Companies must also report dues over 45 days to the ROC every half year in Form MSME-1.

This is often the most effective point to raise with a slow-paying customer's accounts team, because the late payment raises their own tax bill for the year.

How to recover overdue money, step by step

  1. Put the terms in writing. Show your Udyam number, the credit period and a line on interest under section 16 on every quotation and invoice.
  2. Keep proof of delivery. A signed delivery challan, an e-way bill or an email accepting the work fixes the acceptance date.
  3. Send a demand letter once the due date passes, quoting sections 15 and 16 and the interest worked out so far.
  4. File a claim on the MSME ODR portal (odr.msme.gov.in). New delayed-payment cases are filed here; the MSE Facilitation Council first tries conciliation and then decides the case by arbitration.
  5. Hold the buyer to the award. Under section 19, a buyer that wants to challenge an award in court must first deposit 75% of the amount.

What the MSMED (Amendment) Act, 2026 changes

The amendment (Act No. 16 of 2026) is coming into force in stages under Notification S.O. 5575(E) dated 12 October 2026.

FromWhat changes
15 October 2026Free, voluntary MSME registration on a notified national digital platform. Classification by investment in plant and machinery together with turnover. Bills of MSMEs raised on central public sector undertakings to be settled through TReDS, with reporting of those invoices. States can set up more Facilitation Councils. Revised penalties.
15 December 2026New make-up of the MSE Facilitation Councils: three to five members, chaired by an officer not below Joint Director, with an MSE association member and at least one legal member.
Not yet notifiedThe changes to the dispute-resolution and enforcement sections. Until a date is notified, the present process continues.

For suppliers, the practical message is simple. Keep your Udyam registration current, because the 45-day protection depends on it, and expect Facilitation Councils with legal members to handle claims from mid-December.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 45-day rule for MSME payments?

A buyer must pay a registered micro or small enterprise within the agreed credit period, which cannot exceed 45 days from acceptance. With no written agreement, the limit is 15 days. Late payment carries compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate.

Does the 45-day rule apply to medium enterprises?

No. The payment protection under sections 15 to 23 of the MSMED Act applies only to micro and small enterprises with a valid Udyam registration.

What replaced section 43B(h) under the Income-tax Act, 2025?

Section 37(2)(g) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 carries the same rule from tax year 2026-27: a payment due to a micro or small supplier is deductible only in the year it is actually paid, if it is paid after the MSME time limit.

Where do I file a complaint against a buyer who has not paid?

New delayed-payment claims are filed on the MSME ODR portal, odr.msme.gov.in. The MSE Facilitation Council tries conciliation first and then decides the case by arbitration.

What does the MSMED Amendment Act 2026 change from 15 December 2026?

From 15 December 2026, MSE Facilitation Councils will have three to five members, chaired by an officer not below Joint Director, including an MSE association representative and at least one legal member.
